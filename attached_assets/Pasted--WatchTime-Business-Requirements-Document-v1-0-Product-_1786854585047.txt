# WatchTime

## Business Requirements Document — v1.0

**Product:** WatchTime
**Platform:** Responsive Web Application
**Release:** MVP / Open Beta
**Primary language:** English
**Future languages:** Arabic and others
**Business model:** Undecided / deferred
**Mobile apps:** Phase 2+

---

# 1. Executive Summary

WatchTime is a web-based movie and television tracking platform inspired by the former TV Time experience.

The product will reproduce the **core navigation patterns, tracking workflows, information hierarchy, content density, and general UX familiarity of TV Time**, while using an original WatchTime brand, original assets, implementation, design tokens, and codebase.

TV Time historically provided tracking, watchlists, discovery, recommendations, viewing statistics, streaming availability, lists, episode tracking, social interactions and related functionality.

WatchTime's primary competitive advantage is an additional **Content Safety layer** that consolidates information from services such as:

* Common Sense Media
* Kids-In-Mind
* Does the Dog Die?
* CringeMDb
* Approved alternative sources
* Moderated WatchTime community contributions

WatchTime will **not create a single proprietary numerical safety score in the MVP**.

Instead, users receive a quick safety overview followed by clearly attributed source-specific information.

---

# 2. Product Vision

Create the spiritual successor to the TV Time experience for users who valued its simple tracking workflow, while adding a capability TV Time did not make central:

> **Before watching something, users should be able to understand what potentially sensitive content it contains without visiting several different websites.**

WatchTime combines:

**TV Time-style tracking + discovery + Content Safety aggregation.**

---

# 3. Product Positioning

WatchTime is not a streaming service.

Users cannot watch movies or episodes directly through WatchTime.

It is a:

* Viewing tracker
* Personal media organizer
* Movie/TV discovery interface
* Watch-history manager
* Content-information platform
* Content-safety aggregator

# 5. Primary User Types

## 5.1 Visitor

Not logged in.

Can:

* Browse the platform
* Search titles
* Open movie/show pages
* View Content Safety information
* View IMDb / Rotten Tomatoes ratings when legally available
* View streaming availability
* View public statistics

Cannot:

* Track episodes
* Save watch history
* Maintain a watchlist
* Submit Content Safety information
* Save personal state

No guest watch history is stored locally.

---

## 5.2 Registered User

Authenticated through passwordless email.

Can additionally:

* Track watched episodes
* Track movies
* Maintain personal viewing state
* Use WatchTime's TV Time-style tracking experience
* Receive simple recommendations
* Submit qualifying community Content Safety contributions
* Report incorrect Content Safety information
* Submit bugs, feature suggestions, and general feedback

Profiles are **private in the MVP**.

There are no public user profiles.

---

## 5.3 Administrator

The MVP uses **one full-access administrator account**.

The administrator can control:

* Content Safety data
* Provider mappings
* Data synchronization
* Community submissions
* Reports
* Feedback
* Analytics
* Audience statistics
* Failed integrations
* Data-refresh operations

Role-based administration is deferred.

# 6. Authentication

Authentication must use:

**Email OTP and/or Magic Link**

No password is required.

Preferred implementation for the Replit build:

* Clerk passwordless authentication
* Email verification code
* Email verification link

Clerk currently supports both email OTP and email verification links.

Tracking actions should trigger authentication when performed by a visitor.

Example:

Visitor presses:

**Mark Episode Watched**

→ authentication interface appears
→ after authentication, action completes.

The design target is a **high-fidelity functional recreation of the familiar TV Time experience**.

This applies to:

* Navigation patterns
* Content hierarchy
* Card density
* Poster presentation
* Search behavior
* Content detail hierarchy
* Tracking workflow
* Episode browsing
* Watch progress
* Watchlist behavior
* Discovery patterns
* Streaming availability presentation
* General interaction philosophy

WatchTime must use:

* Original branding
* Original logo
* Original assets
* Original code
* Original CSS/design implementation

Do not copy TV Time source code, logos, proprietary artwork, or trademarks.

Where a WatchTime-specific requirement conflicts with historical TV Time behavior, **this BRD takes precedence**.

# 8. Tracking Requirements

Tracking should remain deliberately simple.

## Television

Users can:

* Add a show
* Browse seasons
* Browse episodes
* Mark episodes watched
* Mark multiple previous episodes watched
* See viewing progress
* See the next unwatched episode
* See recently watched content

The product should avoid exposing unnecessary states such as:

* Dropped
* Paused
* On Hold

unless required by the TV Time-inspired baseline.

---

# 9. Movie Tracking

Users can:

* Add movies to the appropriate TV Time-style list/state
* Mark movies watched
* Browse movie information
* View related recommendations
* View Content Safety information
* View external ratings
* View streaming availability

No internal WatchTime star/rating system exists in the MVP.

---

# 10. Search

Search behavior should remain visually and behaviorally close to TV Time.

It must additionally recognize:

* English titles
* Original titles
* Translated titles
* Alternative titles
* AKA titles
* Arabic/localized titles when available

TMDb search supports original, translated, and alternative movie titles.

---

# 11. Discovery

Discovery should reproduce the TV Time-style experience rather than introducing a new safety-oriented discovery interface.

Content Safety filters are **not included in Discover during the MVP**.

Simple recommendations are supported, including concepts such as:

* Similar Titles
* Because You Watched X

A complex ML recommendation engine is unnecessary in the MVP.

---

# 12. Content Detail Page

The content detail page should retain TV Time's approximate information hierarchy.

WatchTime then adds a dedicated:

# Content Safety

section.

Content Safety must not replace or dominate the familiar content page structure.

---

# 13. Content Safety Overview

The top of the Content Safety area should provide a compact summary of available information.

Potential categories include:

* Age suitability
* Sex / Nudity
* Violence / Gore
* Language
* Drugs / Alcohol / Smoking
* Frightening or disturbing material
* Trigger warnings

The summary must be based on available source data.

WatchTime must **not fabricate missing information**.

There is no overall 0–100 WatchTime Safety Score in the MVP.

---

# 14. Source-Specific Safety Information

Below the summary, show information separately by source.

Example:

### Common Sense Media

Age and content guidance

### Kids-In-Mind

Sex/Nudity
Violence/Gore
Language

Kids-In-Mind currently describes these three categories using separate 0–10 measures.

### Does the Dog Die?

Relevant trigger warnings.

The service exposes a broad trigger-warning model and an API.

### CringeMDb

Parent/family awkwardness and relevant adult-content indicators.

CringeMDb currently focuses on identifying films that may be uncomfortable to watch with parents or children.

---

# 15. Attribution

The source name should be visible.

Example:

**Source: Kids-In-Mind**

The external source URL should be accessible from the expanded details rather than cluttering the primary UI.

When sources disagree, WatchTime simply displays the differing information.

It does not:

* Select a winner
* Calculate a consensus
* Display “sources disagree”
* Generate an AI interpretation

---

# 16. Source Hierarchy

Content Safety uses a three-tier hierarchy.

## Tier 1 — Primary Sources

1. Common Sense Media
2. Kids-In-Mind
3. Does the Dog Die?
4. CringeMDb

## Tier 2 — Approved Alternative Sources

Used when primary coverage is missing.

Sources must be explicitly approved before production use.

## Tier 3 — WatchTime Community

Used to supplement missing coverage.

Community information must be clearly identified as:

**Community contribution**

---

# 17. Data Acquisition Rule

Production integrations should use this order:

1. Official API
2. Licensed data feed
3. Explicitly permitted integration
4. Approved alternative provider

Do **not** make unauthorized scraping a core production dependency.

Common Sense Media publishes an API intended for embedding its content in applications.

Rotten Tomatoes requires developers interested in API/data-feed integration to submit a business proposal.

Therefore integrations must support a state such as:

`provider_unavailable`

instead of faking data.

---

# 18. Safety Data Performance Architecture

External Content Safety websites must **never be synchronously queried during normal page rendering**.

Instead:

External source
↓
WatchTime ingestion service
↓
Normalization
↓
WatchTime PostgreSQL database
↓
Cache
↓
User page

This ensures the movie/show page does not wait for four external websites.

---

# 19. Smart Refresh

Content Safety uses adaptive refreshing.

## New Content

Refresh more frequently.

## Recently Released Content

Moderate refresh frequency.

## Old / Stable Content

Refresh infrequently.

## Missing or conflicting records

Move to an accelerated refresh/review queue.

## Administrator

Can manually force:

**Refresh Now**

Refresh intervals must be configurable and not hardcoded throughout the application.

---

# 20. Episode-Level Content Safety

Safety information should use the most specific level available.

Priority:

**Episode → Season → Series**

For movies:

**Movie**

Example:

If episode-specific information exists:

> Episode-specific data

If not:

> Using season-level information

If unavailable:

> Using series-level information

This indicator should be subtle and located within expanded details.

---

# 21. Community Content Safety

Qualified registered users can submit safety information.

A contribution contains:

* Content category
* Severity
* Movie/show
* Season or episode when applicable
* Optional description

Sensitive-scene timestamps are **not included in MVP**.

---

# 22. Community Eligibility

A user cannot immediately contribute after registration.

Two conditions must be satisfied:

### Condition 1

The account must exceed a minimum WatchTime activity threshold.

### Condition 2

The relevant title must have been marked watched by the user.

For an episode-specific contribution, episode-level viewing evidence should increase moderator confidence.

The launch activity threshold should be an **admin-configurable value**, not a permanent hardcoded number.

---

# 23. Community Moderation

All contributions initially receive:

`PENDING_REVIEW`

Only the administrator can:

* Approve
* Reject
* Edit
* Flag
* Remove

Approved contributions become visible publicly.

Public users do not see contributor identities.

They see:

**Community contribution**

The administrator retains the contributor account reference internally for abuse prevention.

---

# 24. Report Incorrect Safety Data

Each Content Safety section must provide:

**Report Issue**

Possible reasons:

* Wrong title matched
* Incorrect information
* Outdated information
* Broken source
* Incorrect episode
* Other

Reports enter the administrator queue.

---

# 25. External Ratings

The MVP target is:

* IMDb
* Rotten Tomatoes

These ratings must only appear when a legitimate data-access method exists.

Do not build production functionality around brittle HTML scraping.

If one provider cannot legally or technically be connected during beta, the UI should gracefully omit it.

* Backdrops

* Genres
* Cast
* Crew
* Seasons

# 33. Internationalization

MVP UI:

**English**

Architecture:

**i18n-ready**

Requirements:

* UI strings stored separately
* No major user-facing strings hardcoded into components
* Layouts should tolerate translated text
* RTL-capable architecture
* Arabic can be added later without rebuilding navigation

Arabic UI itself is not part of the MVP.

# 34. Visual Design

The user wants the familiar TV Time aesthetic.

Therefore prioritize:

* Similar information density
* Similar navigation philosophy
* Similar poster/card proportions
* Similar hierarchy
* Similar interaction placement
* Similar tracking simplicity

But establish WatchTime's own:

* Logo
* Wordmark
* Color tokens
* Icons where necessary
* Branding assets

No separate Light/Dark theme system needs to be created unless required to reproduce the intended TV Time reference experience.

---

# 35. Responsive Design

Web first.

Required screen classes:

* Mobile
* Tablet
* Desktop

Mobile web must be treated as a first-class experience, because native iOS and Android apps are deferred.

---

# 36. Performance Requirements

Target:

* Fast perceived load
* Skeleton loading where appropriate
* Lazy-loading posters
* Image optimization
* Pagination/infinite loading where appropriate
* Cached entertainment metadata
* Cached Content Safety data
* No blocking calls to safety sources
* Database indexes for common searches
* Debounced search
* Server-side aggregation where appropriate

Content detail pages should remain usable when a third-party service is offline.

---

# 37. Reliability

Third-party provider failure must not break WatchTime.

Example:

Common Sense unavailable
→ show cached Common Sense data if valid
→ continue showing other sources
→ log provider failure
→ Admin dashboard indicates issue.

---

# 38. Privacy

Private by default.

MVP contains:

* No public profiles
* No follower graph
* No public viewing activity
* No public identity attached to community safety contributions
* No user-uploaded profile photos

Deletion of personal account information must be supported.

Handling of previously approved anonymous community contributions can be reviewed by the administrator, subject to applicable legal/privacy obligations.

---

# 39. Security

Minimum requirements:

* Passwordless managed authentication
* Secure session handling
* Server-side authorization
* Protected `/admin` routes
* CSRF/XSS/SQL-injection protections
* Input validation
* Rate limiting
* Submission throttling
* API secrets stored only in environment secrets
* Audit logging of important administrator changes

# 45. MVP Acceptance Criteria

The Open Beta is ready when:

1. A visitor can search and browse without creating an account.
2. A user can sign in passwordlessly.
3. A user can track a TV series episode by episode.
4. Progress updates correctly.
5. Movies can be tracked.
6. Movies, TV, anime and documentaries are discoverable.
7. Alternative title search functions.
8. Detail pages follow the approved TV Time-inspired layout.
9. Content Safety appears without blocking page loading.
10. Multiple safety sources can appear independently.
11. Source attribution is visible.
12. Episode → season → series fallback works.
13. Missing providers do not break pages.
14. Qualified users can submit Safety information.
15. Contributions remain invisible until approved.
16. Administrator can manage submissions.
17. Administrator can inspect and repair provider mappings.
18. Users can report Safety issues.
19. Feedback can be submitted.
20. Analytics are recorded.
21. Public statistics contain aggregate data only.
22. Analytics can be exported.
23. Admin routes cannot be accessed by ordinary users.
24. The site works correctly on modern mobile and desktop browsers.

---

# 46. Product Principle

When deciding between adding complexity and retaining the simplicity users remember from TV Time:

> **Preserve simplicity.**

WatchTime's differentiation should primarily come from better information — especially Content Safety — rather than from making the tracking experience more complicated.
